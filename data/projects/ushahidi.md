## What it is

Ushahidi is an open-source platform for collecting eyewitness reports and placing them on a map. Reports arrive by SMS or the web and appear as points that responders can filter.

## The problem

During the 2008 post-election violence in Kenya, information was spread across blogs, news, and phone calls. There was no shared map of what was happening where.

## How the experience works

A report is sent by text message or submitted online. The platform geocodes it, adds it to the map, and lets moderators review and categorize it. The map is the interface; the reports are the data.

## What makes it interesting

Four technologists built it in days as an ad hoc response, not a planned product. It won the NetSquared Mashup Challenge in 2008 and became a platform used in later crises. The SMS input was the key choice: the people closest to an event often have the weakest connection.

## What you can learn

When the network is unreliable, the input channel decides whether the tool works at all. Design for the phone people have, not the one you wish they had.

## Limitations of this analysis

The record does not cover the moderation workflow, verification methods, or later versions of the platform.
