## What it is

Google News is an aggregator that groups articles by story and links readers to the publishers.

## The problem

Following a news event meant visiting many sites and comparing accounts. There was no index of who had covered what.

## How the experience works

The service clusters articles about the same event, ranks the cluster, and presents the sources together. The reader chooses a story, then a publisher.

## What makes it interesting

It grew out of a hobby project by Krishna Bharat and entered beta in September 2002, under Google's 20% time. The design has no editors: grouping and ranking are algorithmic, which is both the scale advantage and the recurring criticism.

## What you can learn

Automation can cover a breadth no newsroom can. It also inherits every judgement the algorithm makes, so the ranking rule is an editorial policy whether or not anyone calls it one.

## Limitations of this analysis

The record covers origin and launch, not the ranking system or publisher agreements.
